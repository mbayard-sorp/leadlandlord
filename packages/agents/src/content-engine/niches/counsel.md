# Counsel Overlay (theme=counsel)

This overlay tunes the content engine for small-town and small-city legal
lead generation: family law, divorce, child custody, personal injury,
bankruptcy, estate planning, DUI, and general criminal defense. The buyer
is in a stressful life moment and is choosing on trust, plain-language
clarity, and "can I talk to a real local attorney today."

## Critical lead-gen constraints (NON-NEGOTIABLE)

This is a lead-gen site, NOT a real law firm. The lead is handed to a
partner attorney who fulfills it. Therefore:

- **No named attorneys, no firm name, no attorney bios, no headshots.**
  The "business name" is keyword + city (e.g. "Boise Family Law"), never
  a partner's name. Hero imagery is civic (courthouse, scales, town
  hall) — never a person.
- **The H1 is the targeted keyword verbatim (ADR 0002)** — e.g. "Family
  Law Attorney in Boise, Idaho". Do not paraphrase it into a slogan.
- **No fabricated reviews, ratings, case results, or settlement amounts
  (ADR 0012).** Do not invent "AVVO 10.0" or "Super Lawyers" — those are
  per-attorney and we do not have an attorney. Omit the testimonial
  section entirely when no real reviews exist.
- **No specific legal advice and no guarantees of outcome.** Frame the
  call to action as a free consultation, not a promise.
- Trust signals are generic and verifiable-in-spirit: "Member, [State]
  Bar", "Free, confidential consultation", "Available 24/7", "No fee
  unless we win" (personal-injury matters only — qualify it).
- Include an attorney-advertising disclaimer and a "not legal advice /
  no attorney-client relationship" line in footer-level copy.

## Terminology

Plain language first; legal terms only where the searcher would use
them.

- Family law: "custody", "parenting plan", "child support",
  "modification", "visitation", "guardianship", "paternity",
  "domestic relations".
- Divorce: "uncontested vs contested", "mediation", "legal
  separation", "property division", "spousal support / alimony",
  "no-fault".
- Personal injury: "car accident", "truck accident", "slip and fall /
  premises liability", "no fee unless we win (contingency)", "statute
  of limitations / deadline to file", "insurance adjuster", "demand".
- Bankruptcy: "Chapter 7 vs Chapter 13", "means test", "automatic
  stay", "debt discharge", "free debt evaluation", "stop wage
  garnishment", "stop foreclosure".
- Estate planning: "will", "living trust", "power of attorney",
  "healthcare directive", "probate", "beneficiary".
- DUI / criminal: "misdemeanor vs felony", "DUI / DWI", "license
  suspension", "expungement / record sealing", "arraignment",
  "first offense".

## Seasonal / Timing Patterns

Legal demand is event-driven, not seasonal — but reflect urgency:

- Divorce inquiries rise January (post-holiday) and September
  (post-summer).
- Personal injury is deadline-driven — emphasize the statute of
  limitations and "call before evidence disappears".
- Bankruptcy rises with economic stress, tax season, and after
  garnishment / foreclosure notices.
- DUI is immediate and time-sensitive (license-hearing deadlines).
- Estate planning spikes around new baby, new home, retirement, a
  death in the family.

## Regulations to Reference Generically

- State bar membership and good standing — "Member, [State] Bar".
- Attorney-advertising rules (every state bar regulates legal ads) —
  hence the required disclaimer.
- Contingency-fee rules for personal injury — "no fee unless we win"
  must be qualified to PI matters.
- Confidentiality / attorney-client privilege — strong reassurance.
- Statutes of limitation by matter type (reference generically; do
  not state a specific deadline as advice).
- Court / county jurisdiction language ("Ada County", "Treasure
  Valley", "[County] District Court").

## Common Customer Pain Points

- Fear and overwhelm — "I don't even know if I have a case."
- Cost anxiety — "I can't afford a lawyer." (Free consult; PI
  contingency; bankruptcy free evaluation.)
- Confidentiality — "Will my spouse / employer find out I called?"
- Speed — "I got served / arrested / in an accident — what do I do
  right now?"
- Being treated like a number — they want a real local person who
  answers, not a call center.
- Plain answers — they want to know what happens next, not legalese.

## Common Objections to Address

1. **"Is the consultation really free?"** — Yes; free and
   confidential whether or not you hire anyone.
2. **"Can I afford this?"** — Free consult; PI is no-fee-unless-we-win;
   bankruptcy free debt evaluation; fees explained before you sign.
3. **"Do I have a case?"** — That's exactly what the consultation is
   for; honest read, no pressure.
4. **"Will I have to go to court?"** — Many matters settle or mediate;
   explained case by case.
5. **"Is this confidential?"** — Yes; strictly confidential from the
   first call.
6. **"How fast can I talk to someone?"** — Reach a real person during
   business hours; after-hours messages returned same day; 24/7 for
   urgent matters.
7. **"What if you can't help me?"** — We'll point you to a local
   attorney who can.

## Tone Notes

Counsel voice is calm, civic, and plain-spoken — reassuring without
hype. Editorial and measured (this is the serif-and-brass variant), but
never cold or stuffy. Lead with empathy for the situation, then the free
consultation and the phone. Use second person. Avoid Latin and jargon
except where the searcher uses it. Never promise outcomes; never
fabricate credentials, reviews, or attorney identities. Every page
should make "talk to a local attorney, free and confidential" the clear
next step.
