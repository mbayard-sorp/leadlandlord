# Haul Overlay (theme=haul)

This overlay tunes the content engine for blue-collar removal and hauling
businesses: junk removal, junk hauling, dumpster rental, light demolition,
debris removal, estate and property cleanouts, appliance and furniture
removal, and moving help. The buyer has a pile of stuff they want gone
today and is choosing on speed, price clarity, and "will they actually
show up and lift it." Content should feel direct, capable, and fast — a
crew that answers the phone and does the heavy lifting.

This is a lead-gen site. There is no named company crew, no fabricated
team bios, and no invented reviews (ADR 0012). Lead with the job, the
phone, and the same-day promise.

## Terminology

Use the words a homeowner or property manager would actually say.

- Junk removal: "single-item pickup", "full truckload", "half load",
  "we load it, you point", "curbside vs full-service", "same-day pickup",
  "haul-away", "volume-based pricing", "no dumpster in the driveway".
- Cleanouts: "garage cleanout", "estate cleanout", "foreclosure /
  eviction cleanout", "hoarding cleanout (handled with discretion)",
  "storage-unit cleanout", "basement / attic cleanout", "whole-house
  cleanout".
- Materials: "furniture", "mattresses", "appliances (fridge, washer,
  dryer, stove)", "e-waste", "yard waste and brush", "construction
  debris", "drywall", "concrete (limited)", "hot tubs", "playsets",
  "shed teardown".
- Demolition: "light demo", "interior demo", "deck / shed / fence
  teardown", "non-structural only", "we haul the debris too".
- Disposal: "transfer station", "recycle and donate when we can",
  "responsible disposal", "we sort it", "donation receipt where
  available".
- Pricing: "free estimate", "upfront price before we load", "no hidden
  fees", "price by volume", "text us a photo for a quote".

## Seasonal Patterns

- Spring cleanouts (March–May): garages, sheds, yard waste after winter.
- Moving season (May–August): move-out cleanouts, leftover furniture,
  landlords turning units between tenants.
- Pre-holiday Q4: declutter before guests, make room for the new couch /
  appliance delivered over the holidays.
- January reset: post-holiday haul-away, New Year decluttering.
- Storm / weather events: debris removal spikes after local storms.
- Real-estate driven year-round: pre-listing cleanouts, post-close
  "previous owner left everything" jobs, estate sales.

## Regulations to Reference Generically

Light regulatory surface — emphasize insurance, responsible disposal,
and reliability over license numbers.

- General liability insurance — name it; customers ask "what if you
  damage my floor or wall on the way out?"
- Workers' comp for crew-based operations — "what if someone gets hurt
  carrying my safe down the stairs?"
- Proper disposal at licensed transfer stations and landfills; do not
  illegally dump. "Responsible disposal" is a real trust signal.
- Appliance refrigerant (Freon) handling rules for fridges / AC units —
  EPA Section 608; mention compliant recovery generically.
- E-waste and electronics recycling rules (varies by state).
- Hazardous materials are generally NOT accepted (paint, chemicals,
  tires, asbestos) — state this clearly to set expectations.
- Local hauling / business license where required.

## Common Customer Pain Points

- "Will you actually come today?" — same-day / next-day is the headline.
- "How much is this going to cost?" — fear of vague or surprise pricing;
  want an upfront number before loading.
- "Do I have to carry it out to the curb?" — no; full-service crews go
  wherever the item is (basement, second floor, backyard).
- "Will you take [mattress / fridge / hot tub / piano]?" — answer the
  awkward-item questions directly.
- "Where does it go?" — landfill vs donate vs recycle; people feel
  better when it's not all dumped.
- "Will you scratch my floors / dent my walls?" — insured, careful,
  blankets and dollies.
- Estate / eviction sensitivity — discretion and compassion matter.

## Common Objections to Address

1. **"Why not just rent a dumpster?"** — No dumpster blocking the
   driveway for a week, no permit, no you-do-the-lifting. We load and
   go same day.
2. **"How do I know the price upfront?"** — Free on-site estimate or
   text-a-photo quote; price locked before we touch anything; no
   hidden fees.
3. **"Can you take everything?"** — Most things: furniture, appliances,
   e-waste, yard waste, construction debris. Clearly list what we
   can't (hazardous materials).
4. **"Are you insured?"** — Yes; general liability and careful crews
   that protect floors and walls.
5. **"How fast can you come?"** — Same-day or next-day in most cases.
6. **"What happens to my stuff?"** — Donate and recycle whenever
   possible; responsible disposal for the rest.
7. **"Do I have to be home?"** — Often no for curbside / garage jobs;
   arrange access and pay remotely.

## Tone Notes

Haul voice is imperative, confident, and phone-first. Short, punchy
sentences. Lead with the action ("We haul it. You point.") and the
same-day promise. Plain blue-collar language — no corporate filler, no
"solutions", no "premium tier". Make the phone number and the free
estimate impossible to miss. Reassure on price clarity, insurance, and
responsible disposal. Confident, not cute.
